Video Calling Tips for Aaja Hosts

Video calling looks simple until you're actually the one on camera, trying to keep a conversation flowing with someone you've just met. The good news is that most of what makes a call feel good comes down to a handful of practical adjustments, not natural talent. This guide covers the setup and conversation habits that make the biggest difference for Aaja hosts.

Getting Your Lighting and Camera Right

Lighting is the single biggest factor in how your video looks, and it costs nothing to fix. Face a light source — a window during the day or a lamp in the evening — rather than having it behind you, which leaves your face dark and hard to see. A few other quick wins:

  • Prop your phone at roughly eye level instead of holding it below your face.
  • Clean your camera lens occasionally — smudges quietly blur your video.
  • Avoid busy, cluttered backgrounds that distract from you.
  • Test your setup with a quick selfie video before going online, especially in a new spot.

It's worth doing this test at different times of day too, since natural light changes significantly between morning and evening. A spot that looks great at noon might look completely different by 8pm, so it helps to know how your setup performs across your actual hosting hours.

Improving Your Audio Quality

Callers often forgive imperfect video more easily than poor audio, since it's harder to follow a conversation you can barely hear. A few habits help:

  • Find a quieter room, away from fans, traffic noise, or a noisy household.
  • Speak at a natural, clear volume — not too quiet, not shouting.
  • If your device's built-in microphone sounds muffled, basic wired earphones with a mic are an affordable upgrade.
  • Avoid handling your phone or tapping the screen while talking, which can create noise on the other end.

If you're not sure how you actually sound, record a short voice memo and listen back. Most people are surprised by small habits, like speaking too close to the microphone or trailing off at the end of sentences, that are easy to fix once you notice them.

Body Language on Camera

Even on a small screen, body language comes through clearly. Sitting up rather than slouching, looking toward the camera lens rather than at your own image, and offering a genuine smile when appropriate all help a caller feel like they're actually being seen and heard. Small nods and natural facial reactions go a long way toward making a call feel like a real conversation rather than a transaction.

It takes a bit of practice to look at the camera lens instead of the screen, since our instinct is usually to watch the other person's face rather than the small dot above or beside it. A little conscious effort here goes a long way toward making callers feel like you're genuinely looking at them.

People respond to warmth and presence far more than perfect delivery. Looking genuinely engaged matters more than being perfectly polished.

Conversation Skills That Help

Good conversation on a call isn't about having a script — it's about staying curious and present. A few approaches that consistently work well:

  1. Ask open questions that invite more than a one-word answer.
  2. Listen for details you can follow up on, rather than planning your next line while the other person talks.
  3. Let silences happen occasionally — not every second needs to be filled.
  4. Match the caller's energy rather than pushing your own mood onto the conversation.

These habits also tend to improve your overall call volume over time — see our guide on how to get more video calls on Aaja for how good calls translate into repeat callers.

Handling Awkward Moments

Every host runs into slow or slightly awkward calls occasionally — a caller who's quiet, a topic that runs dry, or a connection that lags. This is completely normal and not a reflection of your skills as a host. A simple, friendly question or a light comment about something on screen is usually enough to ease things along. If a call becomes genuinely uncomfortable rather than just awkward, it's always fine to end it — our safety tips guide covers this in more detail.

Practicing and Improving Over Time

Video calling is a skill, and like any skill, it improves with repetition. Most hosts notice they feel noticeably more natural on camera after their first couple of weeks of regular calls. Paying attention to what worked well in a good call, and adjusting after a call that didn't go smoothly, builds steady improvement without needing to overthink every interaction.
