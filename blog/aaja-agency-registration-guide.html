Aaja Agency Registration is the starting point for anyone who wants to build a team-based income around the Aaja platform rather than hosting calls themselves. This guide breaks the entire registration process down into clear, practical steps, so you know exactly what to expect from the moment you reach out to support to the moment you start recruiting your first hosts.

Understanding the Agency Role

Before registering, it's worth understanding what the agency role actually involves day to day. An Aaja Agency finds new hosts, helps them get through Aaja Host Registration, and supports them as they learn the platform. In return, the agency earns commission tied to how well its team performs each week.

This is different from being a host yourself. You won't be taking video calls; instead, your income depends on managing and growing a group of people who do. For a fuller picture of the responsibilities involved, see our Aaja Agency Registration page.

Eligibility for Agency Registration

Agency registration is open to most adults, and the bar to get started is intentionally low. Generally, you should be:

  • At least 18 years old
  • Comfortable communicating with people over chat or call
  • Able to reach potential hosts through social media, community groups, or personal contacts
  • Prepared to commit some regular time each week to your team
  • Set up with a bank account or UPI ID for receiving commission settlements

You don't need a company, an office, or any formal business registration to begin. Most agencies start as an individual effort and only formalize later if they choose to.

It also helps to be someone people naturally trust. Since a large part of the agency role involves convincing new hosts to try something unfamiliar, having some existing credibility — whether through a social media following, a community you're active in, or simply a track record of being reliable with people you know — tends to make early recruitment easier. If you don't have this yet, it's not a dealbreaker; it just means your first few hosts may come from closer personal connections before you start reaching a wider audience.

Registration Steps in Detail

The registration process is designed to be simple:

  • Step 1: Message our support team on WhatsApp and mention you're interested in Aaja Agency Registration.
  • Step 2: Share your name, contact details, and how you plan to recruit hosts (this helps our team give you relevant advice).
  • Step 3: Provide your payout details so future commission can be settled correctly.
  • Step 4: Get briefed on the commission structure, host onboarding process, and general expectations.
  • Step 5: Begin identifying and approaching potential hosts, guiding them through their own registration.

Most of this can be completed in a single conversation with support, though building your actual host team takes ongoing effort beyond registration itself.

What Information You'll Need

Agency registration is lighter on documentation than host registration, but a few things speed things up:

  1. A valid contact number and, ideally, a WhatsApp-linked number for ongoing communication.
  2. Bank account or UPI details for weekly commission settlements.
  3. A rough idea of your recruitment plan — even a simple one, like "I'll reach out through my social media followers," helps our team tailor advice.
Agencies that come prepared with even a basic recruitment plan tend to build momentum faster than those who register first and figure out strategy later.

Your First 30 Days as an Agency

The early weeks after registration matter a lot. New agencies who stay active and hands-on with their first few hosts tend to build stronger, longer-lasting teams. Focus on:

  • Recruiting a small, manageable first group rather than trying to scale too fast
  • Helping each new host complete registration and understand daily tasks
  • Checking in regularly to answer questions and resolve early confusion
  • Learning how diamonds and weekly settlements work so you can explain them clearly to your team

Agencies that treat this period as relationship-building rather than a numbers game usually end up with more engaged, longer-term hosts.

Where to Find Your First Hosts

New agencies often ask where to actually find people willing to try hosting. A few starting points that tend to work well:

  • Your existing network: Friends, acquaintances, or extended contacts who are looking for flexible, work-from-home income are often the easiest first conversations to have.
  • Social media groups: Local community groups, part-time job groups, and student groups on platforms like Facebook or WhatsApp often include people actively looking for opportunities like this.
  • Word of mouth: Once you have even one or two active hosts, they often become your best source of referrals, since they can speak honestly about their own experience.
  • Content and visibility: Some agencies build a small social media presence around what they offer, which can attract inbound interest over time rather than requiring constant outreach.

Whichever approach you use, honesty works better than overselling. Being upfront that earnings depend on effort and consistency — rather than promising guaranteed income — tends to attract hosts who stick around, rather than ones who quit after a disappointing first week.

Growing Beyond Your First Team

Once your first group of hosts is active and settled in, growth becomes about repeating what worked and refining what didn't. Some agencies eventually build sub-agencies as their network expands, taking on a more supervisory role over a larger group of recruiters and hosts. If this interests you, our commission structure guide explains how team size and activity can affect your standing over time.

Growth doesn't need to be rushed. A steady, well-supported team is generally more valuable than a large but inactive one, since commission is tied to actual weekly performance rather than headcount alone.

Many agencies find it useful to set simple, personal milestones as they grow — for example, aiming to have a certain number of consistently active hosts before actively recruiting more. This keeps growth manageable and ensures you can still give each host reasonable attention. Trying to onboard too many hosts at once, before you've developed a repeatable onboarding and support process, often leads to a scattered team where nobody gets enough guidance. It's usually better to grow in stages, refining how you support hosts at each size before expanding further.
