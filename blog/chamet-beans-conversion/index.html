Understanding Chamet Beans Conversion

Beans are the in-app balance many Chamet hosts look at first. This guide explains how conversion examples work — and why the numbers must always be checked against current rules.

In this article

  1. What are Beans?
  2. A historical conversion example
  3. How to calculate a conversion yourself
  4. A balance is not the same as withdrawable cash
  5. Where to check the current figures

What are Beans?

On apps like Chamet, virtual gifts received by hosts are commonly credited as an in-app balance, often referred to as Beans. That balance may later be converted to a cash value under the platform’s rules. The conversion rate, minimum amounts and eligibility conditions are set by the platform and can change.

A historical conversion example

Material supplied to us lists a conversion ratio of 10,000 Beans = USD 1. This figure is unverified and historical. We include it only to show how a conversion calculation works.

Illustrative conversion using the unverified historical ratio of 10,000 Beans = $1

Verify the current rate. These values are based on an unverified historical ratio. They are not a confirmed current official rate and must not be relied on for planning.

How to calculate a conversion yourself

If you know the current official ratio, the calculation is simple:

Illustrative value = Beans ÷ Beans per USD 1

For example, using the historical ratio: 250,000 ÷ 10,000 = $25. Replace the ratio with the current verified figure to get a meaningful result.

A balance is not the same as withdrawable cash

Even when you know the conversion rate, a Beans balance is not necessarily money you can withdraw immediately. Withdrawals can be affected by:

  • Minimum withdrawal amounts and permitted increments.
  • Account and identity verification.
  • Supported payment methods and wallet linking.
  • Processing times, fees and adjustments.
  • Account eligibility and compliance with the rules.

Our payment and withdrawal guide lists what to verify.

Where to check the current figures

Check the information shown inside the official app, the platform’s published help or policy pages, or an authorised agency that can point you to official documentation. Be wary of screenshots without a date or source.
