Aaja Agency Commission Structure Explained

Commission is the core of how an Aaja Agency earns, but the structure behind it can feel a little abstract until you see how the pieces connect. This guide explains how commission is calculated, what actually moves you into higher tiers, and how sub-agencies fit into the bigger picture — without guessing at exact numbers that can change over time.

How Agency Commission Works

As an agency, you don't earn diamonds directly from calls the way hosts do. Instead, your commission is calculated as a share of your host team's total weekly diamond earnings. In practice, this means your income is tied directly to how active and successful the hosts you recruited and support actually are.

This is why the agency role is often described as team-based — your effort goes into recruiting and coaching hosts, and your income follows their collective performance. For a broader introduction to the role itself, see our How to Join Aaja Agency in India guide.

It's a fundamentally different earning model compared to hosting directly. As a host, your income is tied to your own individual call time and engagement. As an agency, your income is an aggregate of many individual efforts, which means your role shifts from "performer" to "coach and recruiter." Understanding this distinction early helps set the right expectations about where your time is best spent — less on taking calls yourself, more on finding and supporting the people who do.

What Drives Your Commission Level

A few factors typically influence how much commission an agency earns in a given week:

  • Team size: A larger active host team generally produces more total weekly diamonds.
  • Team activity: An agency with fewer, highly active hosts can sometimes outperform one with many inactive hosts.
  • Host retention: Long-term, engaged hosts contribute more consistently than hosts who churn out quickly.
  • Host progress: As individual hosts move up the host level system, their improved diamond rates also benefit the agency's overall commission.

Understanding Commission Tiers

Aaja Agency commission is generally structured in tiers — as your team's combined weekly performance grows, your agency can move into a higher tier with an improved commission percentage. We deliberately don't quote specific percentages here, since tiers and rates can be updated by the Aaja platform and outdated figures would be misleading.

The safest way to understand your current tier and rate is to confirm directly with our support team — treat any numbers you see elsewhere as a general concept rather than a guarantee.

What we can say confidently is that the general direction is consistent: larger, more consistently active teams unlock better commission terms over time.

Think of tiers less as fixed milestones and more as a reflection of your agency's overall health at any given time. An agency that's growing steadily will naturally trend toward higher tiers as team activity increases, while an agency that loses active hosts without replacing them may see its tier drop accordingly. This is precisely why ongoing host support tends to matter as much as recruitment itself — a tier gained through a burst of recruitment can just as easily be lost if that team isn't retained and supported afterward.

The Role of Sub-Agencies

As an agency grows, some partners choose to build sub-agencies — essentially recruiting and developing other agents who report into their network. This can create additional earning opportunities beyond direct host recruitment, since sub-agents extend your reach without requiring you to personally manage every host.

Sub-agency structures aren't necessary for every agency, and many partners run a successful, direct host team without ever building one. It's simply an option that becomes more relevant as your network matures. Read our Sub-Agency Explained guide for more detail on how this works in practice.

Deciding whether to build a sub-agency network is largely a question of how you want to spend your time as your agency grows. Managing hosts directly gives you closer, hands-on relationships with a smaller number of people. Developing sub-agents shifts more of your time toward mentoring other recruiters, trading some direct host relationships for broader reach. Neither approach is inherently better — it depends on your strengths and what kind of agency you want to build.

How and When Commission Is Paid

Agency commission generally follows the same weekly settlement rhythm as host earnings — weekly performance is totaled, commission is calculated based on your current tier, and the payout is released to your linked bank account or UPI ID. For the full mechanics of the payout process itself, see our Withdrawal Process guide.

Common Misconceptions About Commission

A few misunderstandings about agency commission come up often enough that they're worth addressing directly. First, commission is not a one-time recruitment bonus — it's an ongoing share tied to actual weekly performance, meaning it only continues as long as your team stays active. Recruiting a host and then disengaging doesn't produce lasting income; supporting that host to remain active does.

Second, bigger isn't automatically better. An agency with a small, highly engaged team can sometimes out-earn one with a much larger but mostly inactive roster, since commission tracks real weekly diamond totals rather than headcount. Third, commission tiers are not permanent — they reflect your team's current performance, which means consistent effort is needed to maintain a higher tier, not just reach it once.

Practical Ways to Grow Your Commission

If you're looking to increase your agency commission over time, focus on things within your control:

  1. Recruit hosts who are genuinely interested and available, rather than chasing volume alone.
  2. Support new hosts closely in their first few weeks, since early engagement strongly predicts long-term activity.
  3. Encourage your team to complete daily tasks and weekly milestones consistently.
  4. Check in regularly rather than only reaching out when something goes wrong.

Growth in agency commission tends to follow growth in team health — a well-supported team naturally produces more consistent weekly diamonds.

It's also worth periodically reviewing your team composition rather than only focusing on recruitment. Identify hosts who have gone quiet and reach out to understand why — sometimes it's a simple issue you can help resolve, like confusion around a feature or an unresolved account question. Other times, a host has genuinely moved on, and it's more productive to focus your energy on active members and new recruitment rather than chasing disengaged accounts indefinitely. Treating your host roster as something to actively manage, rather than a static list, tends to produce steadier commission over time.
