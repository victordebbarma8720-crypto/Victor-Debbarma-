How to Register a Chamet Agency: A Step-by-Step Overview

Chamet agency registration is easier to approach when you understand each stage before you start. This overview walks through the general process and the checks worth making at every step.

Before you start: what a Chamet agency does

A Chamet agency arrangement generally involves recruiting eligible hosts, helping them through registration and supporting them while they follow the platform’s rules. It is a management role with real responsibilities, not a passive income scheme. Read Chamet agency responsibilities and rules for a fuller picture of what the role involves.

The exact rights and duties of an agency depend on the program terms that apply when you register. Those terms can change, so treat every guide — including this one — as background information rather than a statement of current policy.

Step 1: Review the current eligibility requirements

Start by finding out what the current program actually requires. Typical questions to answer include:

  • Is there a minimum age or identity verification requirement for agency applicants?
  • Are there country or region restrictions?
  • Does the program expect a minimum number of hosts, or a minimum level of activity?
  • Which terms of service or agency agreement will you be asked to accept?

Write the answers down and keep a note of where each one came from. If the only source is an informal message, treat it as unconfirmed.

Step 2: Verify the registration channel

This is the most important step. Fake “agency registration” links and people charging fees for guaranteed approval are a common risk around live-streaming and video-chat apps. Before you submit anything:

  • Confirm that the registration channel is authorised by the platform, not just by the person who shared it.
  • Be cautious of anyone asking for upfront payments, your account password or one-time codes.
  • Check that any app you install comes from an official app store listing.
Never share passwords or verification codes. A genuine registration process does not need your login password or the one-time code sent to your phone.

Step 3: Submit accurate application information

Provide information that is complete and true. Inaccurate details can delay verification or lead to rejection, and may cause problems later when payments are processed. Use the same name and details that appear on any identity documents you are asked to verify.

Step 4: Complete the required verification

Many platforms verify applicants before approving an agency. This may involve identity checks or account verification. Follow the instructions given through the authorised channel and keep copies of what you submitted. Applying does not guarantee approval.

Step 5: Follow the authorised onboarding procedure

If your application is approved, you will normally receive onboarding instructions. Read them carefully — they usually explain how host recruitment works, what you are responsible for and how commission is reported. Ask questions before you start recruiting, not after.

Step 6: Learn the management system

Approved agencies are often given access to a management tool or dashboard. Learn how it shows your hosts, their status and any commission information. Keep your login details private and enable any security features offered.

Step 7: Understand commission and responsibilities

Before you recruit anyone, make sure you understand how commission is calculated under your program. In general terms, commission equals eligible commissionable revenue multiplied by the applicable rate — but which revenue counts, and which rate applies, depends on the current terms. Our commission explainer walks through illustrative examples.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Using an unverified registration link because it was shared in a group chat.
  • Paying a fee to someone who promises guaranteed approval.
  • Relying on old screenshots of commission tables or conversion rates.
  • Promising hosts specific earnings to recruit them faster.
  • Skipping the terms and finding out about obligations later.
