What Is a DarlingC Agency?

A DarlingC agency is a team responsible for:

  • Recruiting eligible hosts
  • Helping applicants complete registration
  • Providing the correct agency code
  • Reviewing profile photos and videos
  • Training newly approved hosts
  • Monitoring host activity
  • Explaining payment procedures
  • Supporting withdrawal questions
  • Preventing fraud
  • Sharing policy updates
  • Maintaining payment records
  • Managing sub-agencies when permitted

DarlingC Agency Registration Process

  1. Contact an authorized DarlingC agency manager or administrator.
  2. Submit a real name, country and phone number.
  3. Explain recruitment experience.
  4. Share the expected number of host recruits.
  5. Provide required identity or business information.
  6. Review agency rules and commission conditions.
  7. Wait for official approval.
  8. Receive or create agency-dashboard access.
  9. Add an approved payment account.
  10. Start recruiting after approval.

DarlingC Agency Requirements

How to Invite Sub-Agencies

  1. Open the official sub-agency registration link.
  2. Complete the registration form.
  3. Submit genuine personal information.
  4. Send the registered details to the main agency.
  5. Wait for official review and approval.

Note: Sub-agency approval is not automatic.

Agency Payment and Withdrawal

The agency may receive eligible team income through the current settlement system.

  • Correct payment details are required
  • Settlement methods may vary
  • Payment reviews may cause delays
  • Public holidays may affect processing
  • Agencies should keep accurate host-payment records
  • Agencies should never request host passwords or OTPs

Agency Rules and Compliance

Agency managers must not:

  • Create fake host accounts
  • Use false documents
  • Manipulate earnings
  • Share dashboard passwords
  • Mislead applicants
  • Promise guaranteed income
  • Collect unauthorized fees
  • Support account sharing
  • Encourage policy violations
  • Withhold host payments unfairly
Violations may result in commission deductions, restrictions or agency closure.
